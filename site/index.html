Message from the Head of School
校長からのメッセージ
“Our approach is not simply to renovate facilities, but to consider how our physical environment can better support learning.”

The quality of our physical environment plays an important role in the experience of our students and staff. During the past year, we have undertaken significant improvement work across our campus, ranging from classroom and shared-space upgrades to new student facilities, learning spaces and safety improvements.

Our approach is not simply to renovate facilities, but to consider how our physical environment can better support learning, student wellbeing, safety and a strong sense of community. Some projects have already been completed, others are currently underway, and several longer-term priorities have been identified for the coming years.

This plan brings these developments together within one framework and provides a basis for monitoring our progress and planning future investment.

Why it matters ・ 改善が学びを支える理由

How these improvements support learning

Buildings do not teach, but they shape how well teaching lands. Here is how the groups of work above connect to what happens in a school day.

01

A school that feels welcoming

The redesigned reception and entrance change the first thirty seconds of a visit. Families, prospective parents and students arriving late or unwell all meet a space that is calm and clearly organized.

02

Somewhere to belong

The Community Hub, Student Lounge and upgraded counselling room give students places to be outside lesson time — to rest, to talk with friends, or to speak privately with an adult. Students who feel settled take more academic risks.

03

Classrooms that hold attention

Fresh paint, new doors, better-quality student and teacher furniture and a great deal more whiteboard space all reduce friction in an ordinary lesson. More board space means teachers can leave working thinking on display instead of wiping it away.

04

Room for specialists

A dedicated Visual Arts room, new instruments and an improved library mean art, music and independent reading no longer have to borrow space or make do with shared equipment. Specialist subjects grow when they have a home.

05

Comfort and safety students notice

New air-conditioning units, serviced filters and fans, lockers for every age group, organized bicycle parking and the move from pins to magnetic display boards address the practical conditions of the day — temperature, air, where a bag goes, whether a wall is safe to lean on.

06

Teachers well supported

Consistent workstations and improved staff areas, including the Staff Room kitchen, matter to the quality of teaching in a way that is easy to overlook. Staff who have somewhere decent to prepare, mark and take a break bring more to the classroom.
