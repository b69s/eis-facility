Message from the Head of School
Replace the signature block below with your name and title.

Dear Parents and Families,

Over the past year we have carried out the most extensive programme of facility improvement our school has undertaken in some time. The work has ranged from the very visible — a redesigned reception and entrance, a new Community Hub, a Student Lounge — to the quieter but equally important: paint, doors, flooring, furniture, whiteboards, lockers and air conditioning across the Main Building, Early Years Building and Gym.

Our aim throughout has been simple. A school building should feel welcoming to the families who walk into it, comfortable for the students who spend their days in it, and properly equipped for the teachers who teach in it. Each item on the list below was chosen with one of those three things in mind.

The programme is not finished. Curtains, bulletin boards and doors are being replaced now; classroom flooring is scheduled for December; and we are planning a major modernization of the science laboratory over the next two years. This page will be kept up to date as the work continues, and I hope it gives you a clear picture of where the school's investment is going.

As always, I welcome your thoughts. If there is an area of the campus you feel we should be looking at, please do get in touch.

With warm regards,

How these improvements support learning

Buildings do not teach, but they shape how well teaching lands. Here is how the five groups of work above connect to what happens in a school day.

01

A school that feels welcoming

The redesigned reception and entrance change the first thirty seconds of a visit. Families, prospective parents and students arriving late or unwell all meet a space that is calm and clearly organized rather than improvised.

02

Somewhere to belong

The Community Hub, Student Lounge and upgraded counselling room give students places to be outside lesson time — to rest, to talk with friends, or to speak privately with an adult. Belonging and wellbeing are the ground floor of learning; students who feel settled take more academic risks.

03

Classrooms that hold attention

Fresh paint, new doors, better-quality student and teacher furniture and a great deal more whiteboard space all reduce friction in an ordinary lesson. More board space in particular means teachers can leave working thinking on display instead of wiping it away.

04

Room for specialists

A dedicated Visual Arts room, new instruments and an improved library mean art, music and independent reading no longer have to borrow space or make do with shared equipment. Specialist subjects grow when they have a home.

05

Comfort and safety students notice

New air-conditioning units, serviced filters and fans, lockers for every age group, organized bicycle parking and the move from pins to magnetic display boards address the practical conditions of the day — temperature, air, where a bag goes, whether a wall is safe to lean on.

06

Teachers well supported

Consistent workstations and improved staff areas, including the Staff Room kitchen, matter to the quality of teaching in a way that is easy to overlook. Staff who have somewhere decent to prepare, mark and take a break bring more to the classroom.
